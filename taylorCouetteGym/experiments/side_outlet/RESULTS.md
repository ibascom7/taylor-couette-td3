# Results — Yuhe side-outlet run (no RL)

Ran `pimpleFoam` on Yuhe's case **exactly as shipped** (D=0.2 modulated waveform,
endTime 200 s). 1350-cell wedge, OpenFOAM v2506, single core.

- **Wall-clock cost:** 1127 s (~19 min) of solver time for 200 s of physical time.
  Cost is dominated by the **2500 rpm bursts** (Co=1.8 forces Δt≈3.6×10⁻⁴ s there);
  the 16 s off-phase runs at Δt=0.05 s and is nearly free.
- **Physical time simulated:** 200 s (≈ 3 mean residence times τ≈65 s, ≈ 10 modulation cycles).

## 2. Simulation implemented ✅
Mesh `checkMesh` clean (1350 cells, "Mesh OK"). Coded BCs (`lopezModulatedInnerWallVelocity`,
`lopezFullyDevelopedAnnularTopInlet`) and the two coded function objects
(`sideOutletConversion`, `rotationalPower_innerWall`) all compiled and ran.

## 3. Does the dye reach the bottom? — YES, fast. ⏱
- **Breakthrough is essentially immediate: dye is at the bottom side-outlet by the
  first write (t ≈ 4 s)**, i.e. within the very first 2500 rpm burst.
- This is **~16× faster than the mean residence time** (τ = H/U ≈ 65 s). Mean axial
  through-flow is only 0.585 mm/s; the **Taylor vortices generated by the burst
  transport dye axially far faster than the bulk flow**. (See the t=4 s montage
  panel: the burst has already spread dilute dye over the whole height.)
- **Report in "days/seconds": seconds, not days.** Steady state in ~110 s of
  *physical* time = ~19 min of *wall-clock* compute on one core.

## 7. Steady state / warm-up 📈
- The reactor reaches a **periodic (cycle-to-cycle) steady state at t ≈ 110–130 s**
  (≈ 6–7 modulation cycles ≈ 2 residence times). Per-cycle mean conversion:

  | cycle (t-range) | 0 (0–20) | 1 (20–40) | 2 (40–60) | 3 (60–80) | 4 (80–100) | 5 (100–120) | 6 (120–140) | 7–9 (140–200) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | mean X | 0.94 | 0.71 | 0.59 | 0.52 | 0.49 | 0.47 | 0.45 | 0.44–0.47 |

  After cycle ~5 the cycle-mean conversion stops trending and just oscillates
  ±0.015 around **X ≈ 0.45**.
- **⚠ Implication for the RL warm-up:** the existing env uses
  `warmup_duration = 10 s` at 100 rpm. For *this* flow-through case that is far too
  short — the concentration field is nowhere near steady at 10 s. To start RL
  episodes from a steady field, **warm up ≈ 130 s (≈ 7 cycles)**, or until the
  cycle-mean conversion change drops below tolerance.

## 4. ParaView visualization 🎥
`frames/` holds 50 PNGs (every 4 s) of the reactant field `c` on the r–z wedge
face; `montage.png` is the 4-up (t = 4 / 40 / 100 / 200 s) summary. The picture:
dye enters yellow (c₀=50) at the top inlet, the **outer wall stays dark (c=0,
catalytic sink)**, and the high-c zone grows downward over successive bursts until
the periodic steady state.

## 9. Plots 📊
- `omega_vs_time.png` — Lopez waveform: 2500 rpm for 4 s every 20 s, time-avg 500 rpm.
- `energy_vs_time.png` — motor-model power (Eqs 18–23): **total E = 1284 J, avg Pₑ = 6.42 W**
  over 200 s. Per burst: +220 W acceleration-current spike, ~32 W plateau
  (bearing-friction-dominated), −120 W regen-braking recovery at burst end.
- `conversion_vs_time.png` — outlet conversion: starts at X≈0.93 (well-mixed/dilute),
  decays to the steady **X ≈ 0.45** by ~110 s.
- `mixing_index_vs_time.png` — the env's **intensity-of-segregation mixing index**
  applied at mid-height (where the catalytic outer wall sets a radial gradient).
  It is a **sawtooth locked to the bursts**: radial segregation builds during each
  16 s quiescent phase (catalytic wall depletes the near-wall layer) and each
  2500 rpm burst erases it (I_mix → ~0) while stepping the mean concentration up.
  This is the clearest single picture of *how the modulation mixes*. (At the very
  bottom outlet plane I_mix≈0 trivially — that band borders the outlet, not the
  catalytic wall.)

> Note: the CFD `rotationalPower` function object writes at writeTime (every 4 s),
> and every write lands in the **off-phase** (Ω=0), so its samples are ~0 and miss
> the bursts. The energy plot therefore uses the **motor model on the analytic
> ω(t)** (correct, smooth). Fix for next time if CFD power is wanted: log power
> at a fixed small interval (e.g. executeInterval in time steps), not at writeTime.
